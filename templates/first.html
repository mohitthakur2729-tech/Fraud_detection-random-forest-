Abstract

Understanding our approach to securing digital transactions

UPI Fraud is becoming more and more prevalent in financial transactions, and at the same time, frauds are increasing. Conventional methods use rule-based expert systems to detect fraud behaviors, neglecting diverse situations and the extreme imbalance of positive and negative samples. In this paper, we propose a Random Forest-based fraud detection framework to capture transaction patterns learned from labeled data. Each transaction is converted into structured features such as time, category, UPI number, age, amount, state, and PIN code. These features are scaled and evaluated by a supervised Random Forest classifier that combines many decision trees to separate valid transactions from suspicious ones. The model gives balanced predictions for both safe and fraud cases, helping users identify risk from transaction behavior instead of relying only on fixed manual rules.
